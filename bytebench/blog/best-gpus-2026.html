Best GPUs of 2026: Where the Value Actually Sits

Lisa Kim October 24, 2026  ·  13 min read
GPU pricing in late 2026 is in one of its better periods. Supply has stabilized after the volatility of the early 2020s, there are legitimately strong options from both AMD and NVIDIA, and the gap between generations is wide enough to matter. Here's where the actual value sits at each price tier.

I tested ten GPUs in this roundup using a Ryzen 7 9700X paired with DDR5-6000 to avoid CPU bottlenecks. Game suite: Cyberpunk 2077 with RT off (RT results in a separate piece), Baldur's Gate 3, Forza Motorsport 2026, Battlefield 6, and The Finals — a cross-section of CPU-limited and GPU-limited workloads at 1440p and 4K. Power draw was measured at the wall with a TP-Link Kasa smart plug and idle/load temps logged via HWiNFO.

Quick Picks by Price

  • Under $300: RX 7700 XT ($279) — the strongest 1080p/light 1440p card in this range
  • $300–$450: RTX 4070 ($399) — DLSS quality mode at 4K is still a meaningful argument for this price
  • $450–$650: RX 7900 GRE ($489) — AMD's price-cutting has made this the best rasterization value in the mid-high range
  • High-end: RTX 4080 Super ($799) — NVIDIA's best all-rounder; the 4090 still exists but the $/performance gap is hard to justify

Most gaming monitors sold today are 1440p. This is where most buying decisions should be anchored.

The RX 7900 GRE's position is notable — it's trading blows with the RTX 4070 Ti Super at $110 less. AMD's rasterization performance at this tier is genuinely strong. The argument for the NVIDIA card comes down to DLSS 3.5 (Frame Generation) and ray tracing, both of which NVIDIA handles better. If you play games that lean on RT effects, the RTX 4070 Ti Super makes a stronger case. For most games, the 7900 GRE is the smarter buy.

The Budget Tier: RX 7700 XT

At $279, the 7700 XT is the best sub-$300 GPU available right now. It handles 1440p at medium-high settings comfortably (60+ fps in every title we tested), runs quietly on the reference cooler, and the 12GB VRAM buffer means it won't struggle with texture-heavy titles. The RX 6700 XT it replaces had 12GB too, but the 7700 XT is about 18% faster in most workloads at similar or lower power draw.

The NVIDIA equivalent at this price is the RTX 4060 Ti at $299-329. The 4060 Ti has DLSS support, which is worth something, and its lower power draw (165W vs. 245W for the 7700 XT) matters if your case has limited airflow. Performance is close enough that the deciding factor is probably the games you play — DLSS-heavy titles favor NVIDIA, pure rasterization favors AMD.

Mid-Range: RTX 4070 vs. RX 7900 GRE

This is the most interesting bracket right now. The RTX 4070 at $399 has DLSS Frame Generation and solid RT performance. The RX 7900 GRE at $489 has better raw rasterization, more VRAM (16GB vs. 12GB), and sits on a wider memory bus (256-bit vs. 192-bit). Neither is clearly wrong.

My read: if you're on a 165Hz or faster 1440p monitor and want to hit high framerates in fast-paced multiplayer games, the RX 7900 GRE's raw performance advantage justifies the $90 premium over the 4070. If you play slow-paced narrative games or anything with significant RT use, the 4070's DLSS quality and RT performance make it the better choice.

High-End: RTX 4080 Super

The RTX 4080 Super at $799 is the rational top-end pick. It outperforms the RX 7900 XTX in most workloads despite costing $100 more, and the RTX 4090's additional performance (~20% more) isn't worth $600 extra for most people. The 4080 Super also handles 4K gaming at high settings better than any card we've tested in this price bracket — in Cyberpunk at 4K Medium, it averaged 89 fps without DLSS and 128 fps with DLSS Quality.

Power consumption at load peaked at 320W, which is within the range of a good 850W PSU with headroom. The card runs warm — peak GPU temp was 83°C in a case with reasonable airflow — but thermal throttling didn't occur in any of our tests.

What to Watch For

Both AMD and NVIDIA are expected to refresh their lineups in Q2 2027. The RX 8000 series has been leaked extensively and the RTX 5000 series has been in the rumor cycle for over a year. If you're planning a high-end build and can wait 4–6 months, the current GPUs will drop in price when new silicon arrives. If you need something now, the RX 7900 GRE at $489 is the pick that will age most gracefully on a budget — the 16GB VRAM buffer future-proofs it better than the 12GB cards in this bracket.
