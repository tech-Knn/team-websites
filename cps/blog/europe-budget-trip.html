How to Plan a Month-Long Europe Trip on a Shoestring Budget

By Raj MehtaOctober 14, 202611 min read27 Comments

30 days. 14 countries. Under $2,000 all-in including flights. People told me it was impossible. Here's exactly how we did it — and how you can too.

Let me be clear upfront: this wasn't a glamorous trip. There were nights in 12-bed dorm rooms. There were bus rides that lasted nine hours on roads that felt like they were designed by someone who had never been in a vehicle. There was a dinner in Prague that was just bread, cheese, and a very large beer that cost $2.50 total. And it was one of the best months of my life.

The key to budget Europe travel in 2026 is understanding where the value is hiding. Spoiler: it's not in Paris, Amsterdam, or Zurich. But those cities can be done cheaply too, if you're strategic.

The Route: Go East First

The single biggest budget decision you'll make is your route. Western Europe is beautiful and expensive. Eastern Europe is equally beautiful, genuinely fascinating, and costs roughly 40-60% less for everything — accommodation, food, transport, entry fees.

Our route: London (flight arrival, 2 days) → Brussels (1 day) → Berlin (3 days) → Prague (3 days) → Kraków (2 days) → Budapest (3 days) → Ljubljana (2 days) → Zagreb (1 day) → Split (2 days) → Dubrovnik (2 days) → Sarajevo (2 days) → Belgrade (2 days) → Bucharest (2 days) → Istanbul (3 days).

Total budget excluding international flights: $1,200 for everything. International flights (NYC-London, Istanbul-NYC on points + taxes): $320. Grand total: $1,520.

Prague's Old Town Square — stunning and, compared to Western Europe, incredibly affordable.

The Transportation Formula

Eurail vs. Point-to-Point Tickets

This is the question everyone asks and the answer is nuanced. For this Eastern-heavy route, point-to-point tickets beat a Eurail pass significantly. Eurail is worth it if you're doing a lot of France, Germany, Switzerland, and Italy — countries where tickets bought in advance can be expensive.

For Eastern Europe: FlixBus and RegioJet (trains and buses) are often cheaper than Eurail-covered trains. A RegioJet bus from Prague to Kraków: $8. A FlixBus from Berlin to Prague: $12. A night bus from Budapest to Kraków: $15, and you save a night of accommodation.

💡 Pro Tip: Night Trains = Free Accommodation

A night train between two cities you were going to travel between anyway is essentially free accommodation. Book a couchette (6-bed compartment) for $30–40. You sleep, arrive refreshed, save a night's hostel cost. The Budapest–Bucharest overnight, the Paris–Madrid, and the Berlin–Vienna are all classic budget travel moves.

Accommodation: Hostels Done Right

Hostels have changed dramatically. The $8/night nightmare dorms of 2005 still exist, but so do genuinely excellent boutique hostels with private lockers, good wifi, quality mattresses, and social atmospheres that make solo travel feel like a group trip. Budget $15–25/night in Eastern Europe, $25–45 in Western Europe.

Non-obvious tip: hostels with bars are often the cheapest because they subsidize rooms with bar revenue. They're also where you meet the most people. Mosaic House in Prague ($22/night), Retox Party Hostel in Kraków (yes, the name is on-brand, but it's actually great at $18/night), and Wombats in Vienna ($32/night but with a rooftop bar) were our top picks.

Eating on $15 a Day

This sounds impossible in Europe. It isn't, if you know the system:

  • Breakfast: Buy from supermarkets — bread, cheese, fruit, yoghurt. $2–3. This is non-negotiable if you're budget travelling.
  • Lunch: The "lunch menu" at sit-down restaurants is a cultural institution across Eastern Europe. You get a starter + main + drink for $6–10. Order it. Don't order from the dinner menu.
  • Dinner: Street food, markets, or self-catering. Budget $4–7.
  • Beer/Wine: $1.50–3 in Eastern Europe at a bar. Don't drink in tourist areas.

Local markets across Eastern Europe serve incredible food for $3–5 a plate.

The Free Attractions Hiding in Plain Sight

Most of Europe's best experiences are free or nearly free:

  • The first Sunday of every month, French national museums are free (Louvre, Musée d'Orsay, etc.)
  • Berlin's major museums have a "Thursday evening free" policy — check ahead.
  • Prague's old town, Charles Bridge, and astronomical clock cost nothing to experience.
  • Kraków's Kazimierz Jewish Quarter, Rynek Główny square, and the views from Wawel Hill: free.
  • Dubrovnik's city walls: €35 to walk. Worth it. But the view from Mount Srđ (cable car up, hike down) is free and arguably better.
"The most expensive thing about Europe isn't the entry fees — it's the coffee shops you sit in to escape your feet."

The Packing List That Saved Us Money

Carry-on only is a financial superpower. No checked bag fees (adds up to hundreds on budget carriers), no bag drop queues, and you can move faster between cities. Everything we needed for 30 days fit in a 40L backpack:

  • 7 pairs of underwear and socks (quick-dry)
  • 3 t-shirts, 1 button-down, 1 lightweight fleece
  • 2 pairs of trousers (one smart enough for nicer restaurants)
  • 1 pair of shoes (worn on plane), 1 pair of sandals
  • Universal power adapter, portable battery bank, unlocked SIM-ready phone
  • Lightweight rain jacket (essential — Europe doesn't care what month it is)

Budget Breakdown (Per Person, 30 Days)

  • Accommodation (avg $22/night): $660
  • Food (avg $15/day): $450
  • Transport (trains, buses, metro): $230
  • Activities and entry fees: $120
  • SIM card + data: $25
  • Miscellaneous (toiletries, laundry, emergencies): $80
  • Total in-country: $1,565
