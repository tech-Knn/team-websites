Is the Go City Pass Worth It? An Honest Review for Smart Travelers

By James Hartley October 22, 2026 8 min read

Go City passes let you bundle dozens of city attractions into a single prepaid ticket — promising savings of up to 50%. But do they actually deliver? We ran the numbers on five major cities and found the truth is more nuanced than the marketing suggests.

The idea behind Go City is simple: pay one flat fee, get unlimited (or a set number of) admissions to the biggest attractions in a city, skip individual ticket queues, and save money versus buying each entry separately. Sounds ideal. And for certain types of travelers, it genuinely is. For others, it's an expensive mistake.

I've used Go City passes in New York, London, Dubai, Sydney, and Barcelona. Here's what I found.

How Go City Works

Go City offers two main products: the All-Inclusive Pass (unlimited attractions for a set number of days) and the Explorer Pass (choose a set number of attractions from a larger menu). Both are digital — you download the app, scan at each venue, and you're in.

Attractions covered range from the headline tourist sites (Empire State Building, London Eye, Sydney Opera House tours) to lesser-known experiences like cooking classes, harbour cruises, and walking tours. The number of included options varies hugely by city — New York has over 100, smaller cities might have 20.

When the Go City Pass Is Genuinely Worth It

01

You're a First-Time Visitor with a Full Itinerary

If you plan to hit 4–6 major paid attractions in 2–3 days, the All-Inclusive Pass almost always pays off. In New York, the Empire State Building alone costs $44. Add in the 9/11 Memorial Museum ($33), a Statue of Liberty ferry ($25), and Top of the Rock ($40) and you've already exceeded the cost of a 2-day pass before lunch on day two.

02

Cities with Expensive Standalone Attractions

Go City pays off fastest in cities where individual admissions are high — New York, London, Dubai, Sydney. In cheaper destinations, the maths rarely works. A Go City pass in a city where most attractions cost €5–10 each won't save you anything compared to paying as you go.

03

You Want to Skip Lines

The queue-skipping benefit is real and often undervalued. In high season at the London Eye or the Empire State Building, you can wait 45–90 minutes without a pre-booked ticket. The Go City pass includes skip-the-line access at most headline attractions. For peak travel periods, this alone can justify the cost in time saved.

First-time visitors to major cities often get the most value from an all-inclusive city pass.

When It's NOT Worth the Money

Watch out for these scenarios

If you're a slow traveller who prefers to linger (one museum per day, long lunches, wandering), you won't use enough attractions to break even. The same applies if you're returning to a city you know well, or if you prefer free experiences — parks, markets, street art, neighbourhoods — over ticketed attractions.

The Explorer Pass is more flexible but requires careful selection. Not all attractions are equal — some are genuinely top-tier, others are fillers. Always check the full list before buying and calculate whether your shortlisted attractions add up to more than the pass price.

City-by-City Verdict

New York — Strong Buy

The best value city on the Go City network. The attraction list is enormous, prices are high, and the skip-the-line benefit at the Empire State Building, One World Observatory, and the Metropolitan Museum of Art is very real. A 2-day All-Inclusive Pass regularly saves $80–120 for a typical tourist itinerary.

London — Good Value in Peak Season

The Tower of London, London Eye, and Kew Gardens are all included. Strong value if you're visiting in July–August and want skip-the-line access. Less compelling off-season when queue times are minimal and individual tickets are sometimes discounted.

Dubai — Worth It for Families

Dubai's attractions are expensive individually. The Burj Khalifa At the Top experience alone costs AED 149 (around $40). Bundle it with a desert safari, an aquarium visit, and a dhow cruise and the pass saves considerably. Families with children who want to hit multiple theme parks and experiences get the best return here.

Sydney — Situational

Sydney's pass includes the Opera House backstage tour, the SEA LIFE Aquarium, and a harbour cruise. If those three are on your list, you'll break even before you've added anything else. But the must-dos in Sydney — the Harbour Bridge walk, Bondi Beach, the Blue Mountains — are either free or not on the pass, so you need to layer it carefully.

Barcelona — Usually Not Worth It

Barcelona's headline attractions (Sagrada Família, Park Güell) are booked separately with timed tickets and often sell out weeks ahead regardless of pass type. The Go City pass here tends to include secondary attractions. Unless you're doing something unusual, you'll save more by booking individually on the official websites.

Tips for Getting Maximum Value

  • Plan your attractions before buying. List every attraction you actually want to visit, look up individual prices, and compare. Only buy if the pass saves at least 20% — the convenience premium should be worth something too.
  • Buy online, not at the airport. Go City passes bought at airport kiosks or from touts are often marked up significantly. The official Go City website or app is always cheapest.
  • Check what's temporarily closed. If a top-tier attraction is under renovation, the maths can flip. Always verify closures before purchase — Go City does not issue refunds because an attraction was closed.
  • Use it on consecutive days. The All-Inclusive Pass days run consecutively from first use. Don't buy a 3-day pass if you have a day trip planned in the middle — you'll waste a day's value.
  • The Explorer Pass for slower trips. If you're spending a week in a city but only hitting 4–5 paid attractions, the Explorer Pass beats the All-Inclusive. You choose your attractions with no time pressure.
"The Go City pass isn't a blanket recommendation — it's a maths problem. Do the calculation honestly before you buy, and you'll know within five minutes whether it's right for your trip."

Our Final Verdict

Go City is a genuinely useful product for the right traveller: first-timers, families, short stays in expensive cities, and peak-season visitors who want skip-the-line access. It is not a universal money-saver, and buying it without doing the sums first is how travellers end up overpaying.

Bottom Line

Go City passes are worth it when you plan to visit 4+ paid attractions in 2–3 days in a major city with high individual admission prices. Use the Explorer Pass for flexible trips. Always calculate first, buy second. For New York, London, and Dubai specifically — it's often a very good deal.
