Haven Holiday Parks UK: Everything You Need to Know Before You Book

By James Hartley October 26, 2026 8 min read

Haven is the UK's largest holiday park operator with over 40 locations from Cornwall to Scotland. For families looking for an all-in-one British break, it can be exceptional value — but the experience varies enormously between parks. Here's what you need to know before you book.

The Haven model is built around convenience. You arrive, everything is on site — accommodation, entertainment, pools, restaurants, kids' clubs, fairground rides — and you don't need to plan anything. For families with young children, this is genuinely liberating. For couples or adult groups looking for a quiet coastal retreat, it's often the wrong choice.

I've visited five Haven parks across the UK over the past three years. Here's an honest account of what works, what doesn't, and which parks genuinely stand out.

What Haven Gets Right

01

The Location Selection Is Excellent

Haven's parks are genuinely well-placed. Perran Sands in Cornwall sits on one of England's finest stretches of beach. Hafan y Môr in North Wales has direct access to the Llŷn Peninsula coastline. Hoseasons Caister on the Norfolk Broads is in a beautiful area. The properties themselves don't need to be spectacular when you're 200 metres from a beach.

02

The Kids' Club Is Genuinely Good

Haven's Bluey and Bingo kids' entertainment (for younger children) and Attitude activity programmes (for older kids and teens) are professionally run and well-staffed. Children 4–12 get structured daytime entertainment — treasure hunts, craft sessions, staged shows — which gives parents actual free time. For families with children under 12, this alone justifies the price of a Haven break over self-catering accommodation.

03

The Indoor Pools Take Bad Weather Out of the Equation

Every major Haven park has a heated indoor pool. In the UK, where a "summer" holiday can involve three days of rain, this is not a minor point. The pools vary in quality — some are genuinely impressive water complexes with flumes and rapids, others are basic — but they guarantee that bad weather doesn't ruin a week's holiday. For families, this is a significant selling point over self-catering cottages.

Many Haven parks are positioned on or near genuinely beautiful stretches of UK coastline.

What Haven Gets Wrong

The accommodation is Haven's weakest point. Caravans and lodges are functional but rarely inspiring — thin walls, dated interiors, and tight layouts that feel cramped for groups of four or more. The "premium" accommodation tiers (Platinum lodges and Explorer lodges) are genuinely better, with proper kitchens, larger living areas, and more privacy. If you're booking Haven, always go at least one tier above the cheapest option. The difference in experience is significant.

The on-site food is expensive and mediocre. Burgers and chips in the main entertainment complex will cost roughly what you'd pay at a mid-range restaurant in a town, with none of the quality. Always buy groceries for your caravan kitchen and eat out in the nearby town at least once a day. This also saves a significant amount of money over a week.

The entertainment shows in the evening are uneven. Some are surprisingly good — well-produced family shows with professional performers. Others are amateurish. The nightly entertainment schedule is published weekly, so check it when you arrive and plan accordingly.

Our Park Recommendations

9.1/ 10
Perran Sands, Cornwall

The best Haven park in England. Perranporth beach is one of the finest in the country — three miles of Atlantic sand backed by dunes. The park is large but well-managed, with a strong pool complex and reasonable on-site facilities. Book 6+ months ahead for peak summer.

8.7/ 10
Hafan y Môr, North Wales

Beautiful setting on the Llŷn Peninsula. The beach is excellent, the surrounding area has fantastic walking and cycling, and the park itself is one of Haven's best-maintained. Better suited to active families who want to use the park as a base.

8.4/ 10
Rockley Park, Dorset

On the edge of Poole Harbour in the Jurassic Coast area. Brilliant for watersports — kayaking, sailing, windsurfing all on-site. The accommodation is better than average for Haven. The surrounding area offers excellent day trips to Durdle Door, Corfe Castle, and Bournemouth.

7.8/ 10
Primrose Valley, Yorkshire

One of Haven's biggest parks — 1,500+ pitches — which is both its strength (enormous facilities, great pool complex) and weakness (it can feel crowded in peak summer). Filey beach is excellent. Good value off-peak.

How to Save Money on a Haven Holiday

Book Early or Very Late

Haven's pricing model rewards both early booking (12+ months ahead gets you the best availability and sometimes 30–40% cheaper than the same week booked 4 weeks out) and last-minute booking (unsold weeks in the final fortnight are often discounted sharply). The worst time to book is 6–10 weeks before departure — you're paying peak demand prices for poor availability.

Avoid School Holidays Entirely

Haven parks in school holidays (July–August, Easter, half-terms) are dramatically more expensive and more crowded. A week in mid-June or mid-September at most parks costs 40–60% less than the equivalent August week and is significantly quieter. The beach is still good; the entertainment is less packed; the pool is manageable rather than heaving.

Don't Buy the Entertainment Pass at Booking

Haven sells entertainment passes at booking for a fixed fee. In practice, most of the included entertainment (evening shows, mini golf, some kids' activities) is free anyway or available cheaper at the park. The passes only represent value if you're planning to use the paid fairground rides extensively — which is rarely worth it beyond one or two sessions for young children.

"Haven works best when you treat it as a base in a beautiful location rather than as a destination in itself. The beach is the product. The park is just a convenient place to sleep."

Is Haven Right for You?

  • Families with children under 12: Yes, almost certainly. The structured entertainment, indoor pools, and beach access make it very good value for this group.
  • Couples: Depends on the park. Perran Sands or Rockley Park in shoulder season (May, June, September) can be genuinely enjoyable. Avoid large parks in July–August — they're noisy and crowded.
  • Groups of adults: Only if you specifically want a coastal UK base with good amenities. The entertainment is aimed at families; the accommodation in groups of 6+ gets tight.
  • Budget travellers: Off-peak Haven can be very good value — equivalent self-catering accommodation in popular coastal areas often costs more with none of the facilities.
