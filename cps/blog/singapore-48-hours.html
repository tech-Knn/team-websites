48 Hours in Singapore: The Perfect Layover Guide

By TripsyTravelSeptember 28, 20267 min read5 Comments

Changi Airport regularly wins World's Best Airport. The MRT is spotless and on time. The hawker centres serve world-class food for $3. Singapore runs like a machine, and spending 48 hours here is enough to make you want to tear up your onward ticket and stay.

Singapore sits at the crossroads of the world. Almost every long-haul flight between Europe/the Americas and Southeast/East Asia either routes through or near it. A 48-hour layover is one of travel's great underrated moves: most nationalities don't even need a visa for stays under 30 days. The airport will store your big luggage while you explore. The city is that easy.

Day 1: The Classics Done Right

Day 1

Food, Gardens & the World's Most Photographed Skyline

8:00 AM
Maxwell Food Centre: Singapore's most famous hawker centre. Eat Tian Tian chicken rice (always a queue, always worth it), char kway teow from the oldest stall, and end with a cold grass jelly for $1.50.
10:00 AM
Chinatown & Sri Mariamman Temple: Walk the streets, stop at the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple, pick up dried fruit and spices from the wet market. Free.
1:00 PM
Gardens by the Bay: The free Supertree Grove and Cloud Forest (paid, $14 — worth it). The Supertree observation walkway at dusk is one of Singapore's iconic views.
5:00 PM
Marina Bay Sands Skypark: $32 entry to the observation deck. Yes, it's expensive. The 360° view of Singapore at golden hour is worth it once. Skip the infinity pool — it's hotel guests only.
8:00 PM
Clarke Quay: Riverside dinner at one of the outdoor restaurants. Chili crab at Long Beach Seafood is the splurge ($40–60 for two). Or eat at the food stalls along the river for $8 total.

Gardens by the Bay's Supertrees at night — the free light show runs at 7:45pm and 8:45pm daily.

Day 2: The Real Singapore

Day 2

Little India, Kampong Glam & the Best Lunch of Your Trip

7:30 AM
Little India at dawn: Before the tourists arrive. The flower garland sellers, the temple with its morning puja, the teh tarik (pulled milk tea) at a kopitiam for $1.20. This is Singapore's most alive neighbourhood.
10:00 AM
Kampong Glam: The Muslim quarter. Sultan Mosque (free, modest dress required), then wander Haji Lane for its wall murals, independent boutiques, and the best Instagram alley in Asia.
12:30 PM
Lau Pa Sat Festival Market: A Victorian cast-iron market hall in the financial district. Satay alley fires up at lunch — 20 sticks of chicken satay plus peanut sauce and rice for $8.
3:00 PM
Orchard Road & Fort Canning Park: Window shop, then escape into Fort Canning's hilltop green for a quiet hour with a view over the city.
6:00 PM
Departure prep: Head back to Changi. Allow 3 hours minimum. Treat yourself to one last hawker meal inside the airport (T2 food court, S$6) before the flight.

Haji Lane in Kampong Glam — Singapore's most colourful street, best visited on a weekday morning.

"Singapore rewards people who eat at hawker centres, travel by MRT, and resist the urge to spend every meal at a rooftop bar. Do that and you'll leave thinking it's one of the world's most remarkable cities."

Practical Info

Getting Around

The MRT is extraordinary. Get an EZ-Link card from any station ($12 including $7 credit). Tap in, tap out. Trains run every 2–4 minutes at peak times. Air-conditioned. Never late. You will miss it when you leave.

  • Visa: Most nationalities visa-free for up to 30 days
  • Currency: Singapore Dollar (SGD). $1 USD ≈ $1.34 SGD
  • Luggage storage: Changi Airport has 24-hour luggage storage from SGD$4/day
  • SIM card: Buy at the airport arrivals — Singtel Tourist SIM: $15 for 100GB, 7 days
  • Weather: Hot and humid year-round, 28–33°C. Carry a small umbrella — afternoon squalls are common.
  • Budget: $50–80/day covering hawker meals, MRT, 1 paid attraction. Very easy to do cheaply if you avoid hotel bars and tourist restaurants.
