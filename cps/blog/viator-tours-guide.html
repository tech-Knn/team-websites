Why Viator Is the Best Way to Book Tours & Experiences — Plus Our Top Picks

By Sarah Lin October 25, 2026 9 min read

Viator is owned by TripAdvisor and lists over 300,000 tours, activities, and experiences in 190 countries. Used correctly, it's the fastest way to find genuinely memorable experiences in any city — from cooking classes to night safaris to skip-the-line access at the world's biggest sites.

The experience economy has exploded in recent years. Travellers increasingly want to do things rather than just see them — cook with a local family in Bangkok, learn to surf in Bali, explore Rome's catacombs with a historian rather than a printed leaflet. Viator has become the world's largest marketplace for exactly this kind of travel.

But with 300,000 listings, the platform can be overwhelming. Here's how to navigate it efficiently — and our favourite picks from the destinations we know best.

Why Viator Beats Booking Tours Directly

The main argument for booking tours directly with operators is that you cut out the middleman and pay less. In practice, this is rarely true. Viator's scale means it negotiates significant discounts with operators who get consistent volume. Direct booking is sometimes cheaper, but it's inconsistent — and Viator's buyer protection (free cancellation on most bookings, refunds if an operator cancels) is a genuine safety net that direct booking doesn't offer.

The review system is also more reliable than most. Because Viator only allows reviews from verified purchasers, and because TripAdvisor's fraud detection is sophisticated, the ratings are reasonably trustworthy. A tour with 4.8 stars from 2,000+ reviews is a strong signal.

How to Use Viator Like a Pro

01

Filter by "Free Cancellation" First

Most Viator tours offer free cancellation up to 24 hours before the start time. Always filter for this. It lets you book multiple options, then cancel the ones you don't want as your plans crystallise. Travel plans change — don't lock yourself in unnecessarily.

02

Sort by "Traveller Favourites," Not Best Sellers

The default "Best Sellers" sort prioritises commercial performance — the tours Viator makes the most money from. Switch to "Traveller Favourites" to see tours with the highest verified ratings relative to booking volume. This surfaces genuinely excellent smaller operators that might otherwise be buried.

03

Read the 3-Star Reviews

A tour with 4.9 stars but only 30 reviews should be treated differently than one with 4.7 stars and 3,000 reviews. And for any tour you're seriously considering, read a sample of the mid-range reviews. They reveal the real experience gaps — a guide who was knowledgeable but rushed, a tour that overran by two hours, a pickup that was difficult to find. These are the things 5-star reviewers don't mention.

Small-group tours booked through Viator consistently deliver more personal experiences than large coach tours.

Small Group vs. Large Group: Always Go Small

The most consistent predictor of a great Viator tour is group size. Tours capped at 8–12 people almost always receive better reviews than large group alternatives at the same site. The guide can adjust the pace, answer individual questions, and access areas that coaches can't. The premium for small-group tours is usually $10–20 and is almost always worth it.

Look for the "Small group" tag in the listing details, or check the description for explicit maximum participant numbers. If a tour doesn't mention group size limits, assume it's a large coach tour and temper expectations accordingly.

Our Top Viator Picks by Destination

Rome

Vatican Museums & Sistine Chapel — Early Morning Small Group
Highly Rated

Entry before general opening, maximum 12 people. The Sistine Chapel with no crowd is a completely different experience. One of the best-reviewed tours on Viator globally. Book 2–3 weeks ahead in peak season.

Colosseum Underground & Arena Floor Access
Skip the Line

Access areas closed to standard ticket holders. A knowledgeable archaeologist guide makes the history genuinely gripping rather than a list of dates. The underground hypogeum is astonishing.

Tokyo

Tsukiji Outer Market Food Tour — Morning Walk
Best for Foodies

A local guide takes you through the market with explanations of every item, tastings at 8–10 stalls, and a sushi breakfast at the end. This is how you understand Japanese food culture in three hours.

Shinjuku Izakaya Pub Crawl with a Local Guide
Hidden Gem

Tokyo's izakaya culture is invisible to tourists without a local guide. This tour hits three bars in the Golden Gai area, with explanations of ordering etiquette, food pairings, and the social customs around drinking in Japan.

Cairo

Pyramids of Giza — Private Tour with Egyptologist
Top Pick

A licensed Egyptologist guide changes everything at Giza. The history becomes a story rather than a monument. Private format means the guide adjusts entirely to your questions and pace. Worth every penny over a group tour here.

New York

Brooklyn Street Art Walking Tour
Hidden Gem

Bushwick's street art scene is vast and constantly changing. A local artist guide provides context you'd never get alone — who made what, why, and what the neighbourhood looked like before. One of the most reviewed walking tours in New York.

"The difference between a mediocre trip and an extraordinary one is often a single great guide. Viator is the most reliable way I've found to access people who genuinely love what they do."

What Viator Is Not Good For

Viator is a marketplace, not a curator — the quality varies enormously. Some operators treat it as a volume business and deliver perfunctory, rushed tours. Always check: when was the listing last reviewed? A tour with great reviews from three years ago may have changed operators since.

Viator also doesn't cover everything. Truly off-the-beaten-path experiences — hiring a local family to take you farming in rural Laos, a private cooking lesson in someone's home in Morocco — are still better found through local guesthouses, travel forums, or word of mouth. Viator works best for structured experiences in destinations with established tourism infrastructure.

Viator Rewards: Worth Joining

Viator's loyalty programme gives you 5% back in Viator Credits on every booking. Credits don't expire and apply to any future experience. If you book a single $200 tour, you get $10 credit immediately. For travellers who use Viator regularly across multiple trips, this adds up to a free experience every year or so.
