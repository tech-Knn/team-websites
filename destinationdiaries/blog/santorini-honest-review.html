Santorini: An Honest Review After 5 Days of Crowds, Cliffs & Caldera Views

Sofia CabralOctober 16, 2026 · 9 min read

I resisted Santorini for years. Too photographed. Too expensive. Too overrun with cruise ships and Instagram photographers. Then I went. Here's an honest accounting.

The Verdict

Santorini is genuinely spectacular. The caldera — a collapsed volcanic crater filled with the sea, ringed by white-washed villages perched on sheer cliffs — is one of the most dramatic landscapes on Earth. No photograph does it justice. Standing on the rim at Fira at sunset, I understood immediately why the island has attracted millions of visitors. It is, objectively, extraordinary.

It is also, in July and August, an absolute nightmare. Cruise ships disgorge 10,000+ day-trippers daily into a place designed for a fraction of that number. The narrow caldera-edge paths through Oia become shoulder-to-shoulder at any hour. The "famous" sunset viewpoint in Oia involves a two-hour wait and about 2,000 competing photographers.

How to Actually Enjoy Santorini

Go in Shoulder Season

May, early June, or September-October. The crowds drop by 60–70%. Prices follow. The weather is still warm enough for swimming. This is the only genuine advice that matters.

Stay in Firostefáni, Not Oia

Oia is where you go for photos. It is not where you should sleep — the narrow alleys are hellish with luggage and the hotels charge a 40% "Oia premium" for the address. Firostefáni is a 10-minute walk from Fira along the caldera rim, has the same cliff-edge views, and costs significantly less.

Visit the Oia Sunset from the Castle, Not the Main Viewpoint

The Kastro (castle ruin) area at the northern tip of Oia has caldera-facing sunset views and rarely attracts more than 20 people. Why? It requires a 10-minute walk from the main viewpoint. This is apparently too far for most visitors.

Spend a Day on Thirassia

The small island directly opposite Oia, accessible by water taxi, receives approximately 1% of Santorini's visitors and has the same caldera views from the other direction. A traditional fishing village, excellent tavernas, no ATMs, no tour operators, and one small hotel. The most peaceful afternoon I had in Greece.

The Cost Breakdown

  • Caldera-view hotel, Firostefáni (September): €180–280/night
  • Dinner at a mid-range restaurant: €35–60 for two
  • ATV rental (the island's best transport): €35/day
  • Ferry from Athens: €35–50 each way
  • Winery tour (Santorini wines are excellent): €20–40
  • Best Month to VisitMay or September
  • Best Area to StayFirostefáni (caldera views, lower prices)
  • Worst MonthsJuly–August (cruise ship season)
  • Hidden GemThirassia island, accessible by water taxi
  • Best WineAssyrtiko from Santo Wines or Domaine Sigalas

Is It Worth It?

Yes — but only if you visit at the right time and manage your expectations carefully. Santorini will never be an off-the-beaten-path discovery. But the landscape is genuinely transcendent, the wine is excellent, and the caldera at dawn (before the cruise ships arrive) is one of those rare things that exceed the photographs. Go in May. Stay in Firostefáni. Hire an ATV. Wake up early.
