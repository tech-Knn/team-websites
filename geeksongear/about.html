Our Story

We open the box so you don't have to guess

Geeks on Gear started because unboxing videos showed excitement, not information. We wanted actual answers: what's in the box, what's missing, and is the build quality worth the price tag.

Why We Started

The unboxing video problem

If you've ever watched a ten-minute unboxing video to find out whether a product includes a charging brick, you understand the frustration. A lot of unboxing content is about the presenter's reaction to opening the box, not the contents of the box.

We started Geeks on Gear in 2024 to write about what's actually inside consumer electronics packaging: every cable, every adapter, every foam insert. Plus first impressions of build quality, fit, and whether the "premium" packaging justifies the premium price.

We also cover the quietly disappointing trend of brands removing accessories from the box over time — when something used to include a charger and now doesn't, we think that's worth noting. You can make your own call, but at least you should know before you order.

What Matters to Us

How we approach every review

We List Everything

Every item in the box gets named and noted. If something useful is missing, that gets noted too — alongside what comparable products include at the same price.

We Pay Our Own Way

We buy our products at retail prices. When something is sent to us by a brand, we disclose it clearly. A product that arrives free still gets the same critical eye.

No Breathless Hype

Not every product launch deserves "game-changing" or "revolutionary." We write about what we actually observed, not what the press release said we should feel.

We Track Changes Over Time

When Apple removes a feature or Samsung quietly swaps a component in a mid-cycle revision, we note it. Box contents and build quality can change between generations — we try to catch that.

Practical Comparisons

Knowing a product costs $349 means more when you know what else ships in a box for $349. We compare across the bracket, not just against a brand's previous model.

No Embargo Pressure

We don't participate in review embargoes that come with implicit "be nice or lose access" dynamics. We publish when we're ready to publish, with whatever conclusions the product earned.

The Team

Three writers, a lot of packaging material

We're based in different cities but share a single principle: if we wouldn't be honest about a product to a friend's face, we won't publish it.

Jamie Park

Editor & Phones Lead

Jamie has been covering consumer tech since the days of physical keyboards on smartphones. She leads our phone coverage and has a particular interest in what flagships charge extra for compared to the mid-range competition. She has opinions about USB-C cable quality that she will share unprompted.

Sarah Wells

Audio & Reading Devices

Sarah's background is in industrial design, which makes her unusually useful for talking about build quality — she notices when a hinge feels cheap before most reviewers notice there's a hinge. She covers audio gear and e-readers, two categories where build quality directly affects the experience.

Tom Okafor

Gaming & Wearables

Tom covers gaming hardware and smartwatches, which sounds like an odd combination until you realize both categories are full of products that look great in marketing and feel mediocre in hand. He's particularly interested in whether the accessories that ship with a product make it genuinely complete out of the box.

Affiliate & business disclosure
