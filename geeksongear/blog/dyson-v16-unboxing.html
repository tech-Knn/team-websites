Dyson V16 Unboxing: Twelve Attachments, One Confused Reviewer

Jamie Park October 13, 2026  ·  8 min read
The Dyson V16 arrives in a box the size of a small suitcase. Opening it is a nested experience — layers of formed cardboard, each holding progressively smaller items. By the time you reach the bottom, you've arranged twelve separate accessories on the floor and may have temporarily forgotten which end of the vacuum actually cleans things. This is a practical report on what's here, what you'll use, and what you can probably leave in the box.

I ordered the Dyson V16 Absolute ($749) through Dyson's website. "Absolute" is Dyson's highest tier, which means you get the largest accessory bundle. The "Origin" version ($649) and "Plus" ($699) have smaller accessory sets if the attachment count is itself a deterrent.

Everything in the Box — Named and Described

The V16 Absolute ships with twelve items beyond the main vacuum body and charging dock:

  1. High Torque Cleaner Head — the main floor head with motorized brush roll. The one you'll use 80% of the time
  2. Fluffy Optic Cleaner Head — illuminated floor head specifically for hard floors; the built-in laser reveals fine dust and debris invisible to the naked eye. This is the genuinely clever new feature
  3. Laser Slim Fluffy Cleaner Head — smaller than the Fluffy Optic, designed for under furniture and tight spaces
  4. Motorbar Cleaner Head — deep-clean motorized head, denser bristles for carpet
  5. Hair Screw Tool — anti-tangle cylindrical attachment for picking up long hair (designed specifically to avoid the hair-wrapping problem of traditional brush rolls)
  6. Combination Tool — dual-mode: dust brush one end, crevice tool the other. The most versatile small attachment
  7. Crevice Tool — elongated narrow nozzle for edges, vents, sofa gaps
  8. Stiff Bristle Brush — for upholstery, car interiors, stubborn embedded debris
  9. Soft Dusting Brush — for surfaces you don't want to scratch: bookshelves, electronics, lampshades
  10. Mini Motorized Tool — small powered brush head for stairs, upholstery, pet hair on furniture
  11. Wand Storage Clip — attaches to the dock so the wand extension stores tidily when not in use
  12. Charging Dock with wall mount hardware — includes two machine screws and two wall anchors; Dyson includes a template card for alignment

For storage, the dock has a built-in holder for three attachments. The remaining nine need to find a home on their own — Dyson sells a separate floor dock with more attachment storage for $49, which I suspect exists specifically for V16 Absolute buyers who discover the included dock is not proportionate to the accessory count.

Which Ones You'll Actually Use

After two weeks of actual vacuuming (this article was written post-testing for the unboxing impression, follow-up review covers performance), my honest breakdown:

  • Use constantly: High Torque Cleaner Head (main carpeted floors), Fluffy Optic Head (hardwood and tile — the laser is genuinely useful), Combination Tool (everything else)
  • Use occasionally: Hair Screw Tool (if you have long hair or pets), Mini Motorized Tool (stairs, couch), Crevice Tool (vents, car interior)
  • Rarely or never touched: Motorbar Head (redundant with High Torque for most surfaces), Stiff Bristle Brush, Soft Dusting Brush, Laser Slim Fluffy Head

That's six attachments used regularly, three used occasionally, three rarely touched. Dyson would argue you're paying for the flexibility to use all of them; I'd argue they're including a selling point that most buyers won't fully use.

The Fluffy Optic Head

The standout inclusion at first use is the Fluffy Optic head with its built-in laser. The laser illuminates a horizontal plane across the floor at a low angle, which makes dust, crumbs, hair, and debris visible that you'd otherwise miss under normal lighting. The effect is genuinely startling — hardwood floors that looked clean under overhead lighting showed a visible particle layer under the laser. This isn't a gimmick; it's a practical feature that affects cleaning thoroughness.

Build Quality Impressions

The V16 body is lighter than the V15 — Dyson claims a 5% weight reduction. The balance point is in roughly the right place for extended use; I didn't experience wrist fatigue in a 20-minute whole-apartment session. The clickable attachment system continues to work reliably; no attachments loosened during use.

The clear dustbin shows fill level without removing it, which is useful. The hygienic point-and-shoot emptying mechanism (press the bottom button over a bin) works cleanly without requiring contact with debris — this is a premium feature that less expensive cordless vacuums don't offer.

The $749 Question

The Dyson V16 Absolute is expensive. A Miele CX1 canister vacuum or a Shark NeverLost APEX upright costs $300-400 less and arguably cleans better in some configurations. The argument for the V16 is the cordless convenience, the laser illumination feature, and the build quality. Whether those things are worth a $300-400 premium is personal. The unboxing, at least, reflects the price: the construction is solid, the accessories feel premium, and the dock is simple to mount.

The Box Verdict

Twelve attachments is more than most homes need, but the core tools are excellent — especially the Fluffy Optic with its laser illumination. The box includes a charging dock with wall mounting hardware, which is a thoughtful practical inclusion. If the attachment count intimidates, consider the V16 Origin at $649, which comes with fewer accessories but the same core vacuum. Full performance review forthcoming.
