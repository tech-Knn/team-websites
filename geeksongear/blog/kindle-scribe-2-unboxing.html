Kindle Scribe 2 Unboxing: Amazon Finally Includes a Better Pen

Sarah Wells October 7, 2026  ·  6 min read
The original Kindle Scribe shipped with a pen that required a battery — a AAA battery, inserted into the barrel of the stylus. For a premium device positioned as a notebook replacement, this felt like an oversight that Amazon took two years to fix. The Scribe 2 ships with a completely redesigned stylus that charges from the device itself via a magnetic snap connector on the side. This is the single biggest practical improvement in the box.

I ordered the Kindle Scribe 2 (16GB, $369) through Amazon. The outer box is Amazon's standard e-reader packaging — matte black, minimal graphics, Kindle logo. The Scribe 2's box is noticeably thinner than the original Scribe's box because the new pen doesn't require a cradle insert.

Everything in the Box

  • Kindle Scribe 2 — 10.2-inch e-ink display, feels immediately familiar if you've used a Kindle Paperwhite
  • Redesigned stylus (Basic Pen) — a flat-sided barrel that snaps magnetically to the right edge of the Scribe 2. No battery required; charges from the device magnetically. The previous "Basic Pen" required a AAAA battery (not a typo — quadruple A, a battery you probably don't have at home). This new pen is a substantial quality-of-life improvement
  • USB-C charging cable (0.5m) — short. Amazon's choice to include a half-meter cable is puzzling; it's too short to use on a nightstand unless your outlet is immediately adjacent. You'll want a longer cable
  • Quick start guide

No power adapter, same as the original Scribe. No cover or protective sleeve, which Amazon sells separately starting at $49.

The New Pen in Detail

The redesigned stylus is 17.2cm long with a flat edge that lines up with the Scribe 2's charging strip. Magnetic attachment is strong enough that the pen doesn't slide or detach when you pick the Scribe 2 up by the pen — I tested this specifically because the original cradle attachment was a common complaint. The pen holds at a right angle to the body, which means it adds only 5mm to the effective width of the device when stored.

The pen charges when attached to the device and the device is awake, drawing from the Scribe 2's battery. Amazon claims 20 minutes of attachment gives 1 hour of writing time. The tip has a slight texture that simulates paper-on-pen resistance — similar to Remarkable's feel but slightly smoother. It takes about a week of regular use to feel natural if you're coming from writing on paper.

The "Basic Pen" vs. "Premium Pen" distinction continues. The Premium Pen ($79, also sold separately) adds an eraser function on the top end and a shortcut button on the barrel. The Basic Pen in the box has neither, which means erasing requires switching modes in software. For annotation of documents (PDF markup, note-taking alongside reading), the Basic Pen is sufficient. For deep note-taking workflows, the Premium Pen earns its upgrade.

Device First Impressions

The Kindle Scribe 2's display is 10.2 inches at 300 PPI — unchanged from the original Scribe's resolution, but the frontlight has been updated. The warm light mode is noticeably warmer than the original, and the transition from cool to warm is smoother. Auto-adjusting frontlight (which responds to ambient light sensor data) is also more accurate in this generation — the original had a tendency to overcorrect in mixed lighting.

Weight is 433g — 5g lighter than the original Scribe. The weight reduction is imperceptible in use. The device is still on the heavy side for a dedicated e-reader (a standard Kindle Paperwhite is 213g), which is the inevitable trade-off for the larger display and writing surface.

Setup: sign in with Amazon credentials, library sync begins immediately. If you've been reading on Kindle across devices, your library is available within a minute. The writing notebooks app opens automatically on first boot and walks through a brief stylus calibration (draw dots at the corners of the screen). Calibration took about 2 minutes.

The Short Cable Issue

The 0.5m USB-C cable is my main practical complaint. Most other devices include 1m cables because 0.5m is often too short to reach from a bedside outlet to a comfortable position on a nightstand. Amazon includes 0.5m here, presumably to keep costs down. A replacement 1m USB-C cable is $7-10; just order one with the Scribe 2. It's a minor annoyance, not a problem with the device, but it's worth knowing before you open the box expecting a cable you can immediately use.

The $369 Context

The Kindle Scribe 2 competes with the Remarkable 2 ($299 device, $7.99/month subscription for cloud features) and the Supernote A5 X2 ($449). Amazon's advantage is the Kindle ecosystem integration — your existing Kindle library transfers immediately. The subscription structure is more consumer-friendly than Remarkable's (Scribe 2 cloud sync is free with a standard Amazon account). The pen improvement narrows the gap with Remarkable 2's stylus feel, though Remarkable's paper-like texture is still slightly better in our testing.

The Box Verdict

The biggest change from Scribe 1 to Scribe 2 is literally in the box: the new stylus without a battery requirement is a practical improvement that Amazon should have shipped in the original. The 0.5m cable is a minor annoyance worth knowing about. The device hardware is evolutionary rather than revolutionary — if you have a Scribe 1, the upgrade is primarily for the pen. If you're buying a first Scribe, the 2 is the right choice. Full review after extended daily use.
