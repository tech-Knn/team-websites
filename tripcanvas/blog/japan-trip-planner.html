Japan Trip Planner: Everything You Need for an Unforgettable Visit

Marcus ChenOctober 24, 2026 · 14 min read

Japan routinely tops lists of the world's best travel destinations — and it lives up to the billing. But planning a Japan trip well requires more research than most destinations. Here's everything I've learned from five visits across different seasons.

When to Visit Japan

Spring (March–May): Cherry blossom season is the most famous and most crowded. Sakura typically peaks in late March to mid-April in Tokyo and Kyoto. Book accommodation 4–6 months ahead if you want cherry blossom season.

Autumn (September–November): Fall foliage season rivals spring for beauty and crowds, with slightly cooler weather. Often the best season for first-timers who miss spring timing.

Winter (December–February): Ski season in Hokkaido and Nagano. Much cheaper accommodation. Some temples and gardens are breathtaking in snow.

Sample 10-Day Japan Itinerary

Days 1–4: Tokyo. Shibuya crossing, Senso-ji, Tsukiji outer market breakfast, Harajuku, a day trip to Nikko or Kamakura. Four days barely scratches the surface.

Days 5–6: Kyoto. Fushimi Inari at 5am before crowds, Arashiyama bamboo grove, Gion district at dusk. Stay in a ryokan for at least one night.

Day 7: Nara. Day trip from Kyoto — the deer park, Todai-ji temple, and Naramachi district. Leave by 3pm to beat crowds.

Days 8–9: Osaka. Dotonbori food crawl (takoyaki, okonomiyaki, kushikatsu), Osaka Castle, Kuromon Market. Best street food city in Japan.

Day 10: Return to Tokyo via Shinkansen. Stop at Mount Fuji area (Fuji Five Lakes) if weather is clear.

JR Pass: Buy or Skip?

The JR Pass is worth it if you're doing the Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka circuit plus one or two additional long-distance legs (Hiroshima, Hokkaido, etc.). For most 10-14 day visitors on that classic route, it pays for itself.

If you're staying primarily in Tokyo and Kyoto, do the math — Suica card (IC card) and point-to-point Shinkansen tickets may be cheaper.

Japan on a Budget

Japan has a reputation for being expensive that is somewhat exaggerated. The costs that catch people off guard: accommodation (plan $50–80/night for decent private rooms in major cities), and pre-trip spending on rail passes. Daily food and transport are genuinely cheap — a bowl of ramen costs $8–10, a convenience store bento lunch $5, and subway rides $2–3.

The cheapest way to sleep: capsule hotels ($25–40) in cities, and business hotels slightly outside city centers.
